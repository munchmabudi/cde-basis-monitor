Modeling assumptions — read before trusting a number

This tool has not been calibrated against real market data yet — the CDE basis monitor's own log is only a few weeks old, nowhere near enough to have lived through genuinely different volatility regimes. Everything below is a reasonable starting assumption, not a measured fact:
  • Mean-reversion speed, volatility, and correlation are estimated, not fit to real history. As the monitor accumulates more data, these should be re-calibrated against actual observed behavior.
  • Maker rebate pool uses a placeholder for the total market's monthly maker volume, since that's not something we can observe from our own data alone — only your own maker volume. Adjust "assumed total market monthly maker contracts" if you get a real number.
  • FRONT_BACK legs (both legs are dated futures, no perp involved) are modeled with the same 1-maker + 1-taker fee structure as the perp-involving pairs for simplicity. In reality both legs of a front-back spread could potentially be executed as maker, which would change that pair's economics somewhat.
  • Slippage is not separately modeled — per your stated execution approach (maker on the illiquid leg, taker hedge immediately after fill), it's assumed to be small and effectively captured within the stated fee structure. If real fills show meaningfully more slippage than that, costs here are understated.
  • "Frequency of new opportunities arriving" isn't a separate input — it emerges from the volatility, correlation, and mean-reversion settings together (with 51 simultaneously-evolving spreads, something crossing the entry bar is usually just a matter of time). Avg rolls/month is reported as an output, not set directly.
